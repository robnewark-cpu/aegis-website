Application management best practices for teams that cannot afford downtime

Most enterprises do not fail because they lack software. They fail because the applications they already run drift: unpatched, undocumented, owned by nobody, and connected in ways nobody mapped. Application management is the discipline of keeping those systems reliable after go-live.

Own the inventory before you optimize it

Start with a living inventory: what runs, who owns it, what it integrates with, and what happens if it stops. Aegis treats this as a baseline, not a one-time audit. Without it, patching, access reviews, and vendor renewals are guesswork.

Separate run, change, and risk

  • Run — monitoring, backups, job schedules, and on-call paths that actually page a human.
  • Change — a written path from request to production, including who can approve data-model or permission changes.
  • Risk — known defects, end-of-life platforms, and privileged accounts, reviewed on a cadence rather than after an incident.

Patch with a calendar, not a hope

Unplanned “we’ll get to it” patching is how ransomware and silent data corruption arrive. Bundle operating-system, runtime, and application updates into a published window. Measure coverage, not activity. If a system cannot be patched, document the compensating control and a retirement date.

Instrument what users actually feel

CPU charts are not user experience. Track login success, job completion, page time for the three workflows that make money, and error rates on integrations. When those signals degrade, application management has failed even if the servers look idle.
